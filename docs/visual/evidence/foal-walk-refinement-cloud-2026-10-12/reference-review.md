# Real walking references inspected for foal refinement

Inspected on 2026-10-12. These are animation reference observations, not medical assessments or calibrated biomechanical measurements. Main contact sheets are extracted from the original media and labeled with original video frame presentation timestamps (PTS). Source speed may differ from real time when the source is slow motion.

- Source: https://www.horsesinsideout.com/post/understanding-assessing-your-horse-s-movement-part-1-the-biomechanics-of-walk
- Video: https://video.wixstatic.com/video/5c2ee3_e4b4ea2e93a14bdbb86bf12afdc99a10/720p/mp4/file.mp4
- Credit: Gillian Higgins / Horses Inside Out. Publicly embedded educational video, used here for reference only; do not redistribute the full media as an original asset.
- Local media: hio-adult-walk.mp4 (164.01s, 51.8MB, 25fps).
- Actual sheets viewed: hio-adult-overview.jpg, hio-adult-12-16s.jpg, hio-adult-50-54s.jpg.
- The 12–16s interval is a normal-speed in-hand walk; 50–54s is a slower playback side-view sequence.
- Visible: the recovering foreleg folds briefly, then extends forward and lowers; hoof clearance is modest relative to limb length. The whole foreleg does not remain high during forward reach. Stance legs trail as the trunk moves over them. Hind limbs recover forward under the body. Individual contacts are staggered rather than a simultaneous diagonal trot-pair action; no aerial phase is visible in the sampled sequences.

## Application to the rig
Use the adult compact foreleg recovery/low forward reach as the stronger visual reference, while retaining juvenile proportions and a modestly shorter step. Narrowing a visibly splayed rig can be evaluated artistically against compact under-body stepping, but the chosen track widths and knee angles must be described as rig adjustments, not measurements established by these videos. Preserve hoof contact and avoid crossing the midline or adding a trot-like diagonal synchronization.
