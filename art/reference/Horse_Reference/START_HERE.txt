An editable, textured horse reference adapted from the CC0 model by Lyndon
Daniels. The proportions, face and main body topology originate in that
authored model. This package updates the materials, object organization,
scale, hoof separation and basic posing rig, and supplies consistent views.
Your existing Horse project and meshes have not been changed.
Saved and checked with Blender 5.2.1 LTS.
GLB import checked with Godot 4.7.2-stable (official).

SCALE AND AXES
One Blender unit is one meter. Withers height: 1.60 m.
The hoof soles meet ground height 0. Object origins sit at ground beneath
the barrel. Blender uses Z up / -Y front; glTF and Godot use Y up / +Z front.
Positive X is the horse's anatomical left. The model shows an adult horse
in a relaxed standing pose.

All six cameras are orthographic and share a 2.80 m vertical span.
The PNG images have the same frame and scale: 731.43 pixels per meter.
No individual view has been enlarged to fill its panel. Front and rear views
are deliberately narrower on the sheet. Grid lines are 0.25 m apart.

USING IT TO IMPROVE YOUR BLENDER HORSE
1. Open your existing file, then use File > Append. Select this .blend and
   select Horse_Geometry and Horse_Rig together in its Collection folder and
   append both in one operation. Keep your
   own horse in a separate collection and save a new working copy.
2. Compare in orthographic side, front and top views. Match overall size and
   silhouette before adding fine surface detail. Measure height at the
   withers, not the ears or poll.
3. Check the balance of shoulder, barrel and hindquarters. A useful starting
   guide is roughly equal withers height and shoulder-to-buttock body length.
   The back should read as a shorter bridge between the shoulder and hips;
   the underside rises toward the flank.
4. Make the shoulder, elbow, knee, cannon, fetlock, pastern and hoof legible
   in the front legs. In the hind legs the stifle sits forward and the hock
   points backward. The hock is an ankle joint; avoid treating it as a knee.
   Preserve four distinct legs when checking the front/rear silhouettes.
   Continue the pastern slope into the front hoof wall; avoid an abrupt boot
   shape. Fore hooves generally have rounder toes, while hind hooves are
   narrower and more pointed.
5. Compare the poll, cheek/jaw, eye sockets, throatlatch and muzzle. Give the
   muzzle real width in the front and top views. Keep eyes on the sides of
   the head and the ear shells hollow-looking, with a defined inner surface.
6. Keep loops around bending joints and concentrate useful geometry at the
   shoulder, hip, stifle, hock and fetlock. Avoid a dense triangulated sculpt
   as your editable base. Inspect this model's control cage in Edit Mode;
   its smoothing is a separate Subdivision Surface modifier.
7. Append the 3D reference for the easiest alignment. If you instead add a
   view PNG as an Image Reference, its full-frame world width is
   3.50 m and height is 2.80 m. Place it at the shared
   camera center recorded in view_calibration.json, then align the image
   plane with the corresponding camera in this Blender file.

BLENDER EDITING AND EXPORT
The body has an editable quad-dominant cage with a non-destructive smoothing
modifier. Hooves, mane/forelock, tail and eyes are separate objects. UVs are
retained from the base where applicable. Check UV reuse before painting new
asymmetric markings; any new hoof geometry may need its own unwrap for a
future textured hoof material. Packed maps let this .blend open on its own.

To export after edits: return the armature to its rest pose, select all model
meshes and Horse_Rig, File > Export > glTF 2.0, choose GLB, Selected Objects,
Apply Modifiers, +Y Up, Materials and Skinning. Keep cameras and lights out
of the game export. Preserve your editable subdivision cage in the .blend.

GODOT 4
Copy Horse_Reference.glb into your project's asset folder. Let Godot import
it, then drag it into a Node3D scene. Use a wrapper node for gameplay movement,
collision and scripts. The visual front is +Z, following Godot's asset
convention. If your controller treats -Z as forward, rotate the visual child
180 degrees around Y. Do not change the skeleton bind pose to fix facing.

The actual export contains 36,162 triangles and 27 armature bones.
All exported elements have skin weights. This is a simple FK posing skeleton:
rotate parent bones in Pose Mode, then refine the rig and weights for your
specific animations. No walk, trot, canter, IK foot locks, production control
rig, gameplay controller or collision setup is supplied. Test extreme poses
and locomotion yourself before using it as an animated hero asset.

The isolated Godot import check confirms a usable imported scene, meshes,
textures, normals, skin links and skeleton. It does not establish animation
quality or performance in your existing game. Exact reports are included.
